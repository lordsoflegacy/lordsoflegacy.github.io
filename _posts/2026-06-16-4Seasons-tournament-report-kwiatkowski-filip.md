## The deck

I played 8-cast.
Or Affinity.
Or Artifact Stompy, or Artifacts Blue.
Whatever you want to call it.

There are many variations to the "core build" as of right now:
- Some people play Chalice of the Void.
- Some people play Tamiyo.
- Some people play Thought Monitor, Krang, or Cori.

I decided to play a version that I like to call _Legendary Affinity_.
The _legendary_ part distinguishes this version
by introducing the addition of Mox Amber.

Mox Amber is not a strong Legacy card.
It's not a card this version was built around.

It's a card that was added because of another addition
--- Tamiyo.

Originally, I wasn't a big fan of trying out Tamiyo.
I thought that generating multiple clues,
consistently flipping it by cracking Baubles in a timely manner,
and potentially being an efficient blocker was either lackluster
or outright suited to be labeled as a _magical Christmas land_.

I was wrong.
Very wrong.

This proves to me that I ought to always test every card
that _could_ work.

My list played a playset of Tamiyos, two Mox Ambers,
and precisely zero Force of Wills.

Force of Will is a strong card
--- don't get me wrong,
I don't doubt that.
It's just too big of a card disadvantage
to be frequently worth it in game 1.

And if game 2–3 is when it theoretically shines
(or at least you know that you should mulligan for it,
e.g., against combo decks),
why not play 0-mana, 2-for-1 counters exclusively in the sideboard?

That's what I did.
I substituted 4 Force of Wills with 4 Metallic Rebukes.

Rebuke is much, much better than Force in fair matchups,
while being noticeably, but not catastrophically
worse in combo matchups.

Besides, how many combo decks can I face
in the field where decks to beat are:
UR Cori, UB Tempo, and Tron?

## Side event

I started things off with the Super Legacy side event.
6 rounds total, payouts for $$ 4-2 $$ or better,
depending solely on points,
not standings.

### Round 1 --- Doomsday

I'm on the draw against a super-friendly gentleman from China.
He plays Underground Sea $$→$$ Thoughtseize.
Then cantrips, and Doomsdays with protection,
which concludes game 1.

> sideboard

Game 2 he drops Hexing Squelcher and Doomsdays,
but I am able to create two Saga Constructs quickly enough
so that after fetching Skateboard,
they both can attack for lethal.

Noteworthy --- if I had Lavaspur Boots instead of Skateboard,
I would've lost that game.
That's because Skateboard tapped the Squelcher,
where Boots-equipped Construct would have just faced being blocked.

Game 3 opponent loses some life to fetchlands, Thoughtseizes,
me attacking for 3 with Memory Guardian (or 3 Drones --- not sure),
and ultimately Doomsdays while being at 8.
I cast Sink into Stupor that acts as basically a counterspell here,
but the opponent fetches in response and counters it with Red Elemental Blast.

Nevertheless, that turned out to be not enough for him.
He is missing exactly 1 life to be able to set up a pile that wins immediately.
I'm not certain about the details,
but that's what he told me, at least.
Maybe it involved putting a fetchland in the pile,
drawing it, and thinning out the deck.

Either way, he loses that game,
and I am officially 1--0.

### Round 3 --- BUG Aluren

I'm on the draw and see a Tropical Island $$→$$ Ponder.
I assumed I'm yet again playing against a combo deck.

I was right.
Just not right about the kind of combo deck.

I had put my opponent on UG Omnitell,
but he turned out to be piloting BUG Aluren.

Game 1 I won through a grind --- I cast Rebukes,
created Saga Constructs, generated value with Tamiyo
(Clues are great at either replenishing resources
or being there as extra artifacts for Improvise or Constructs).

Very relevant --- I managed to cast Metallic Rebuke by tapping two Clues,
which I then sacrificed on the same turn to draw cards.

I wonder how many of you knew
that Clues do not require tapping
in their activation cost :>

> sideboarding

Game 2 is also a grind.
I recall countering like 3 Alurens.
Well, the last counter was Sink into Stupor,
but it acted as a final blow.

Opponent showed me his hand and explained how he would've won the game
if I hadn't ~~countered~~Sinked his last Aluren.

> insert Agony of Defeat

Yet again Sink turns out to be a gamechanger.
I'm loving it.

Oh, but you got to win somehow.
My notes say that I had Pinnacle Emissary and Memory Guardian.

## Main event

Then I cast Memory Guardian.

He then untaps and Reanimates Griselbrand.

and cast Sink into Stupor targeting his Griselbrand.

> sideboarding

### Round 5 --- Affinity

Ah yes, finally, a fair matchup.
Took me only 8 matches.

This is an Ancient Tomb, Patchwork Automaton, and Chalice of the Void build,
which I learn of quite quickly,
because my opponent plays T1 Sink into Stupor $$→$$ Chalice of the Void at 1,
and T2 Ancient Tomb $$→$$ Patchwork Automaton.

Fortunately, Kappa Cannoneer,
which I cast on my T3,
is a bit bigger of a threat
than his Patchwork.
I note a single attack for 14,
9 of which were thanks to Kappa equipped with Shadowspear.

This puts the game out of his reach.

I noticed that his play patterns suggested
that he could've countered Kappa,
but chose not to.
I asked him about this later,
and he confirms.
His rationale was that he had hoped for outgrinding me.
Huh.

> sideboarding

Game 2 starts quite tough,
since I keep a hand with Emry and two 0-drops,
and stare at it while being on the draw
and witnessing my opponent casting Chalice of the Void at 0.
Of course, after deplying 0-drops of his own.

Well...

He then proceeds to cast his Emry, Lurker of the Loch.

I consider already thinking about game 3,
but:

> This is my first fair matchup of the event,
> I'm playing it out,
> even if just for the sake of it.

Things slow down.
To the point where I am being attacked by his Emry.
Twice.

I must've cast Kappa Cannoneer,
because my notes say that he Force of Wills something.

After some time I draw Pinnacle Emissary,
and decide that I need to develop some Drones.

I cast my 0-drops announcing my Emissary triggers every time,
after which my opponent just looks at me and says _okay_.

This is where things get slightly uncomfortable.
The exact sequence of plays were as follows:

+ I move Mox Opal from my hand onto the battlefield,
  announce that I'm casting it,
  and immediately announce my Emissary trigger.
+ Opponent says _okay_.
+ I put a Drone token, pause, and look at my opponent.
+ 2 seconds go by.
+ I move Urza's Bauble from my hand onto the battlefield,
  announce that I'm casting it,
  and immediately announce my Emissary trigger.
+ Opponent says _okay_.
+ I put a Drone token, pause, and look at my opponent.
+ 2 seconds go by.
+ I attempt to tap my land to cast Kappa,
  but the opponent stops me and says
  that my 0-drops are countered.

I'm not sure if this enumeration faithfully represents
the exact dynamic between me and my opponent.
Nevertheless, I begin to explain why I believe he missed Chalice triggers,
but soon after we call a judge (rightfully so).

Judge listens to both of us,
speaks with my opponent in Italian for a bit,
and then rules that I do get to keep my 0-drops.

My opponent doesn't seem happy about this (unsurprisingly),
and mentions that in the past he had experienced
judges ruling otherwise.

I don't delve into the details,
because I don't see the point.
Maybe he was telling the truth.
Maybe he misremembered something crucial.
Maybe the judges
(potentially including the one
who intervened this match)
made wrong calls.

I feel a bit bad,
because my opponent was super friendly,
and this situation clearly caused his mood to shift.
I hope that he will not act upon his words
that he will not be playing Legacy again,
because he truly seemed like a great guy.

Okay, enough of the slight off-topic.
I cast Kappa and win.
